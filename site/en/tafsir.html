Tafsir

The Tafsir connector opens thirteen classical works of Qur'anic exegesis to AI assistants. Ask about a verse and the assistant shows what each exegete says about it, from the work's own text, with the work and the verse unit.

Works

In order of their authors' deaths: al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan · al-Maturidi, Ta'wilat al-Qur'an · al-Zamakhshari, al-Kashshaf · Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, Mafatih al-Ghayb · al-Qurtubi, al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an · al-Baydawi, Anwar al-Tanzil · al-Nasafi, Madarik al-Tanzil · Ibn Kathir, Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim · al-Jalalayn · al-Suyuti, al-Durr al-Manthur · Abu al-Su'ud, Irshad al-'Aql al-Salim · Isma'il Haqqi Bursawi, Ruh al-Bayan · al-Alusi, Ruh al-Ma'ani.

How it works

  • Each work is divided into verse units; the unit of citation is the sentence. For a verse the assistant first gives the places in the Risale-i Nur tied to it, by name and kind of tie, then the exegetes in order of death.
  • Texts are in their original Arabic. If you do not read Arabic, the assistant adds its own translation after the quotation and says it is an AI translation, not a published one.
  • Editors' and editions' introductions (in the al-Maturidi and al-Qurtubi editions) are a separate layer, never attributed to the author.
  • Two exegetes treating the same verse is not a relation between them. A relation the assistant notices is its interpretation and stands under its own heading. Nothing the assistant writes is called tafsir; tafsir is the exegete's work.
  • The text of the Risale-i Nur is not on this connector. Add the Risale-i Nur connector too and the assistant reads it there.
