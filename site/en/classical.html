Classical Works

The Classical Works connector opens to AI assistants the classical Arabic works the Risale-i Nur refers its reader to and relies on: rhetoric and language, theology (kalam) and legal theory, Sufism, the Prophet's life. The assistant shows them from their own text, with the work and the section, and knows where and how the Risale-i Nur names each one.

Works

In order of their authors' deaths: Abu Hanifa, al-Fiqh al-Akbar · 'Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani, Dala'il al-I'jaz and Asrar al-Balagha · al-Raghib al-Isfahani, al-Mufradat fi Gharib al-Qur'an · al-Ghazali, al-Munqidh min al-Dalal and Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din · al-Sakkaki, Miftah al-'Ulum · Sa'd al-Din al-Taftazani, al-Talwih and Sharh al-Maqasid · al-Sayyid al-Sharif al-Jurjani, al-Ta'rifat and Sharh al-Mawaqif · al-Qastallani, al-Mawahib al-Laduniyya.

Where the Risale-i Nur names them

The works were chosen from the Risale-i Nur's own references. For each work the assistant knows where the Risale names it and how; it reads those passages with the Risale-i Nur connector.

  • Referred to for the details: Sharh al-Mawaqif and Sharh al-Maqasid (the Thirty-Third Word, Muhakemat), Dala'il al-I'jaz and Asrar al-Balagha (Isharat al-I'jaz, Muhakemat), the end of the Miftah (Isharat al-I'jaz), al-Mawahib al-Laduniyya (Shuaat Ma'rifat al-Nabi).
  • Named or discussed: al-Talwih (its "Twelve Premises" on destiny, the Twenty-Eighth Letter), al-Fiqh al-Akbar (Sünuhat).
  • Not named: al-Munqidh (al-Ghazali is often cited, not this work), al-Mufradat and al-Ta'rifat (reference works of words and terms). The assistant says so.
  • The Ihya': Bediüzzaman does not name it, but the publisher's notes to some eighty hadiths in the Risale-i Nur cite it; those whose wording appears word for word in the Ihya' are linked to the place.

How it works

  • The unit of citation is the sentence. Sections are the edition's headings; the Dala'il is divided into its editor Mahmud Muhammad Shakir's numbered paragraphs, al-Mufradat into roots, al-Ta'rifat into chapters of the alphabet. In Sharh al-Mawaqif and al-Talwih the headings come from the Shamela table of contents, in Sharh al-Maqasid from the text itself. In unpunctuated editions (Sharh al-Maqasid, al-Talwih) a "sentence" is a long paragraph.
  • Texts are in their original Arabic. If you do not read Arabic, the assistant adds its own translation after the quotation and says it is an AI translation, not a published one.
  • The editor's introduction (Shakir's, in the Dala'il) is a separate layer, never attributed to the author. In Sharh al-Mawaqif, al-Iji's base text is also a separate layer where the edition marks it (المتن), apart from Sayyid Sharif's commentary. In the Ihya', al-'Iraqi's notes on the hadiths are kept apart from al-Ghazali's words, as commentary on the sentence they explain.
  • Where the Risale names a work is recorded; which passage of the work it relies on mostly is not. A relation the assistant notices is its interpretation and stands under its own heading.
  • The text of the Risale-i Nur is not on this connector. Add the Risale-i Nur connector too and the assistant reads it there.
